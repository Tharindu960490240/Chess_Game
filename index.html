    How to play chess

    The goal

    Trap the other player's king so it can't escape. That's called checkmate, and it wins the game. The king is never actually taken.

    White always moves first, then the players take turns, one move each. You can't skip a turn.

    Controls in this game

    • Move a piece: tap it, then tap a square. Or drag it there.
    • Dots show where the piece can go. Rings show pieces you can capture.
    • A piece you can't move won't pick up, so the game never lets you make an illegal move.
    • Turn the board: drag an empty area. Scroll or pinch to zoom. The reset button puts the camera back.
    • Stuck? Press Hint. Made a mistake? Press Take back.

    How each piece moves

    The number is the piece's rough value, which helps you judge a trade. Gold dots are squares it can reach.

    To capture, move onto the square of an enemy piece and it's removed. Only the knight can jump over other pieces.

    Special moves

    • Castling tucks your king into safety. Move the king two squares toward a rook, and the rook hops over to the king's other side. It's only allowed if neither piece has moved yet, nothing stands between them, and the king isn't in check or crossing an attacked square.
    • Promotion: a pawn that reaches the far side becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight. You choose, and almost everyone picks a queen.
    • En passant: if an enemy pawn moves two squares and lands right beside your pawn, you can capture it as if it had moved only one. You must do it on the very next move.

    Check, checkmate and draws

    • Check: your king is under attack, and its square glows red. You must fix it right away by moving the king, blocking the attack, or capturing the attacker.
    • Checkmate: the king is in check and there's no way out. The game ends.
    • Draw: nobody wins if the player to move has no legal move but isn't in check (stalemate), neither side has enough pieces left to checkmate, 50 moves pass with no capture or pawn move, or the same position appears three times.
    • With a clock, running out of time loses the game. You can also resign.

    Tips for your first games

    1. Start with a centre pawn, like the pawn in front of your king moving two squares.
    2. Bring out your knights and bishops early. Keep the queen home for the first few moves.
    3. Castle early to keep your king safe.
    4. Before every move, ask: what is my opponent threatening?
    5. Don't give pieces away for free. Use the values above to check a trade.
    6. Play the computer on Easy with legal-move dots turned on, and use Hint whenever you're unsure.
